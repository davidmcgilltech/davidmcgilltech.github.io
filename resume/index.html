A resume that lists job titles tells you where someone sat. This page tells you what I have actually shipped and what I built it with, which is the part that matters when you are deciding whether to hire me.

Systems I have built

  • Multi-tenant SaaS platforms. Organizations sign up, invite their own users under role-based permissions, and see only their own data. Includes payment processing, automated messaging, and live reporting dashboards.
  • Field operations tools. Job orders, vehicle and asset tracking, crew scheduling, and assignment workflows for contractors whose staff work away from a desk all day.
  • Native iOS applications. Swift and SwiftUI apps for teams that record structured observations in the field, including audio capture, transcription, and evaluation against a scoring framework.
  • Document generation pipelines. Systems that extract structured data out of PDFs and rate tables and produce branded, client-ready documents from it.
  • Voice and email agents. Inbound call and email handling that connects to the ticketing systems a helpdesk already runs on.
  • Public websites with an owner-controlled admin. Schedules, rosters, sponsors, and content the client edits themselves, with no monthly platform fee.

Tools I use

Frontend: TypeScript, React, Next.js, Tailwind CSS, server-rendered HTML where a framework is not warranted.

Backend: Python with FastAPI, Node and TypeScript, .NET, PostgreSQL, Supabase, Cloudflare Workers and D1.

Mobile: Swift and SwiftUI, distributed through TestFlight and the App Store.

Integrations: Stripe for payments, Twilio for SMS and voice, Microsoft Graph, and the REST APIs of whatever CRM or ticketing system the client already pays for.

Testing: Playwright for browser automation, pytest and unit suites, and continuous integration that runs the whole suite on every change.

The quality assurance half

I work in quality assurance on high-traffic sports media properties. The job is finding the exact way a release breaks before millions of people find it instead. That background is why my projects ship with real test coverage rather than a promise of it, and why I am more interested in what happens when your network drops mid-form than in how the happy path looks in a demo.

How I work with clients

Fixed fee, quoted before work starts. Payment by milestone, so you see working software before the next invoice. Full ownership at handoff, including source code, database, and hosting accounts. A defined support window after launch. If a project is a bad idea, or too small to be worth paying me for, I will tell you that instead of taking the work.
