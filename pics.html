Who you are hiring

I am the person who writes your code. There is no account manager between us, no offshore team you never meet, and no handoff to a junior developer after the contract is signed. When you send a message about a bug, it reaches the person who can fix it.

My day job is quality assurance on high-traffic sports media properties. That work is mostly finding the specific way software breaks under real traffic, on real devices, for people who are not being patient about it. It has made me a different kind of developer than someone who has only ever shipped features. I assume things will break, and I build so that when they do, the failure is small and visible instead of silent and expensive.

How I got here

I started taking on freelance projects for local businesses that were paying monthly for software they did not own and could not change. A restaurant renting an ordering system. A shop renting a scheduling tool. Every one of them wrote a check every month for something that would vanish the day they stopped paying.

That is still most of what I do. A business describes a process they run on spreadsheets, sticky notes, or an expensive subscription, and I build them a system that does it properly and belongs to them when it is finished.

Outside of work

I am a dad and a pilot. Flying taught me the habit that shows up most in my work: run the checklist every time, especially when you are sure you do not need to. Software written by someone who skips the checklist looks fine right up until it is not.
